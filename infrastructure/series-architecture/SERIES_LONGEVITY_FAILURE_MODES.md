## PURPOSE

This document catalogues structural failure modes that can damage or end a fiction series. It is a diagnostic reference. Not every series is exposed to every mode. The Contract identifies which modes are most relevant.

Each entry: Definition, Mechanism, Symptoms, At-risk types, Prevention, Recovery.

---

## FM-01: PREMISE EXHAUSTION

**Definition:** The series's core premise has been fully explored with no new angles.

**Mechanism:** The premise was finite but treated as renewable. All variations explored; series continues from momentum.

**Symptoms:** Books feel like variations on an exhausted theme; author struggles to find new angles; distinctive element now just background.

**At risk:** Open-ended with finite premises; recurring adventurer with explored role; recurring setting with exhausted dynamics.

**Prevention:** Identify whether the premise is genuinely renewable before declaring open-ended. Build a renewable engine not dependent on the premise alone. Apply the Interest Diagnostic: is the renewable resource generating interest or just incidents?

**Recovery:** Find a new engine; accept the series should end; spin off.

---

## FM-02: REPETITIVE SEQUELS

**Definition:** Books become formulaic with cosmetic variation.

**Mechanism:** Successful formula repeated without evolution, deepening, or disruption.

**Symptoms:** Readers predict structure; reviews mention "more of the same"; books blur together.

**At risk:** Episodic series; recurring adventurer; recurring setting with static characters.

**Prevention:** Use the Variation Matrix. Vary escalation modes. Let characters evolve. Plan each book to do something previous books haven't. Apply the Deepen/Vary/Complicate/Progress Test.

**Recovery:** Deliberately break the formula; introduce structural disruption; shift escalation mode; accept formula has run its course.

---

## FM-03: SEQUEL INFLATION

**Definition:** Stakes/scale must be bigger each book, creating unsustainable spiral.

**Mechanism:** Ascending escalation as primary mode. Each resolution requires bigger threat. Scale exceeds credible support.

**Symptoms:** Stakes absurd (world → universe → reality); power inflation; earlier books trivial; intimacy lost; author struggling to escalate.

**At risk:** Series using ascending escalation; action/adventure; fantasy/sci-fi with power systems.

**Prevention:** Use non-ascending modes (deepening, lateral, consequential, revelatory). Pair ascending with a ceiling. Vary escalation type (emotional, moral, structural) not just scale.

**Recovery:** Reset scale deliberately; shift mode; accept ceiling reached; reframe "bigger" as deeper/more complex.

---

## FM-04: ACCIDENTAL DEPENDENCY

**Definition:** Low-dependency series accumulates continuity until books can't be read independently.

**Mechanism:** Each book adds canon; later books reference earlier events; drift from Tier 1 to Tier 2/3 without conscious decision.

**Symptoms:** New readers confused at Book 5; author must check earlier books; increasing "previously on" exposition.

**At risk:** Episodic series running many books; recurring setting; hybrid with episodic surface and progressive understructure.

**Prevention:** Monitor continuity drift. Close threads. Compartmentalise. Build recap in. Introduce new entry points.

**Recovery:** Write a standalone book; acknowledge drift and accept; spin off new entry-point series; reclassify as sequential.

---

## FM-05: CANON OVERLOAD

**Definition:** Accumulated canon constrains the author and burdens the reader.

**Mechanism:** Books add canon faster than they close or compartmentalise. History becomes an obstacle.

**Symptoms:** Author spends significant time checking continuity; new characters need extensive backstory; world feels cluttered; contradictions appear.

**At risk:** Long-running series; progressive and sequential; extensive worldbuilding.

**Prevention:** Close threads. Compartmentalise. Use reset moments. Distinguish "must honour" from "nice to know" canon.

**Recovery:** Time jump; cast change; setting change; soft reboot; accept the burden.

---

## FM-06: PROTAGONIST EXHAUSTION

**Definition:** Protagonist fully explored — growth complete, backstory revealed, relationships established, nothing new to discover.

**Mechanism:** Protagonist was a finite resource treated as renewable. All depths plumbed, secrets revealed, growth completed.

**Symptoms:** Protagonist feels static; nothing new revealed; emotional responses predictable; author finds them less interesting; secondary characters more engaging.

**At risk:** Fixed protagonist series; evolution arc (growth vector IS the engine); recurring adventurer with accreting permanence.

**Prevention:** Identify growth vector and pace it. Leave dimensions unexplored. Give protagonist evolving relationships. Consider cast rotation before exhaustion.

**Recovery:** Introduce a new dimension; shift to co-protagonist; let completed growth create new capacity; accept exhaustion and end.

---

## FM-07: STATUS-QUO PARALYSIS

**Definition:** Series can't change because the reader promise depends on things staying the same.

**Mechanism:** Series appeal tied to a specific configuration. Change would violate the promise. But without change, the series can't evolve.

**Symptoms:** Books reset to baseline; meaningful change introduced and undone; series feels frozen; author feels trapped.

**At risk:** Recurring setting; episodic series; cozy/traditional genre with stability expectations.

**Prevention:** Design controlled evolution within the stable framework. Distinguish what must stay stable from what can evolve. Let the promise include deepening.

**Recovery:** Introduce controlled change; accept the series needs to end; shift the promise (risky); spin off.

---

## FM-08: MYTHOLOGY INFLATION

**Definition:** Series mythology keeps expanding without adding story value.

**Mechanism:** Each book adds mythological elements to maintain interest. Old elements not resolved; new ones piled on. Mythology becomes burden.

**Symptoms:** Mythology more complex than story needs; readers need glossaries; new revelations feel like retcons; mythology contradicts itself.

**At risk:** Overarching saga; fantasy/sci-fi; hidden-history structures.

**Prevention:** Budget mythology: decide how much the series needs. Resolve threads rather than expanding. Ensure each element serves story. Let some mysteries remain mysterious.

**Recovery:** Consolidate; simplify; resolve; accept complexity and reboot.

---

## FM-09: PREMATURE CLIMAX

**Definition:** Series reaches its dramatic peak too early. Later books feel anticlimactic.

**Mechanism:** Protected resource spent too early, driven by desire for dramatic peak.

**Symptoms:** Most memorable moment in early/middle book; later books feel like denouement; series winding down when it should be building.

**At risk:** Overarching saga with ascending escalation; single central mystery; eager-to-reveal authors.

**Prevention:** Identify and pace protected resources. Plan dramatic peak near the ending. Use non-ascending escalation so "biggest" isn't the only measure. Resist playing the biggest card early.

**Recovery:** Find a different kind of peak (emotional, moral, structural); accept and plan graceful landing; introduce new dimension; accept damage and end.

---

## FM-10: ENDLESS MIDDLE

**Definition:** Series runs without approaching its ending. Middle stretches indefinitely.

**Mechanism:** Bounded/approximate series treated as open-ended. Author doesn't commit to destination.

**Symptoms:** Many books with no sense of approaching ending; distance to ending doesn't decrease; readers losing patience; complications added to delay.

**At risk:** Bounded/approximate series that became commercially successful; sagas where author doesn't want to give up the world; central question author is afraid to answer.

**Prevention:** Declare natural ending condition before Book 1. Plan pacing. Commit to ending. Resist commercial temptation to delay.

**Recovery:** Commit to ending and book count; accelerate; plan strong finale; accept lost readers.

---

## FM-11: EXTENSION BEYOND NATURAL ENDING

**Definition:** Series reached its natural ending but continues.

**Mechanism:** Central question answered, growth complete, saga resolved — but series continues. Author/publisher not ready to end.

**Symptoms:** Central conflict resolved; new conflicts feel manufactured; series lost purpose; books written because series exists, not because stories exist.

**At risk:** Any series at its natural ending; commercially successful series; series with passionate fanbases.

**Prevention:** Declare natural ending condition. When met, decide: end or find genuinely new engine. If new engine, ensure it's generative, not continuation of old. Be honest about whether there's more to say.

**Recovery:** Find genuinely new engine; accept and plan finale; spin off rather than extend; accept damage and end.

---

## FM-12: READER-PROMISE DRIFT

**Definition:** Series became something different from what Book 1 promised.

**Mechanism:** Series evolves in tone, scope, genre, or structure without conscious decision to change the promise.

**Symptoms:** Reviews mention the series "changed"; early and late books feel like different series; tone shifted; structural promise changed; readers dropping off.

**At risk:** Long-running series; author interests changed; genre conventions pulling toward escalation.

**Prevention:** Articulate reader promise before Book 1. Monitor across books. If changing, make it conscious and documented. Distinguish deepening (same core, more depth) from changing (different core).

**Recovery:** Return to original promise; consciously evolve and communicate; accept drift and commit to new promise; end and start new series.

---

## FM-13: COHESION LOSS

**Definition:** Series becomes so loosely connected it loses identity as a series.

**Mechanism:** In rotating-protagonist, shared-world, and anthology series, the linking element is too thin to create series identity.

**Symptoms:** Readers don't perceive books as a series; books could be standalone novels; linking element so broad it applies to unrelated books; author struggles to articulate what makes them a series.

**At risk:** Anthology; rotating-protagonist; shared-world with minimal continuity.

**Prevention:** Ensure linking element is specific enough to create identity. Create recurring anchors (location, character, institution, tradition). Let world accumulate. Give readers reason to read as a series.

**Recovery:** Introduce a recurring anchor; deepen world continuity; accept it's a collection and reposition; strengthen future connections or end.

---

## FM-14: EXCESSIVE CAST ACCUMULATION

**Definition:** Characters added faster than retired. Cast bloats; focus dilutes.

**Mechanism:** Each book introduces new characters; none leave. Cast grows beyond what the story needs.

**Symptoms:** Recurring cast too large; POV characters proliferate; new characters introduced but old ones not retired; readers ask "who is this again?"

**At risk:** Ensemble; saga; shared world/rotating.

**Prevention:** Cast rotation policy (1 in, 1 out after threshold). Focus characters designated per book. Consolidate redundant roles.

**Recovery:** Retire characters; consolidate roles; accept the cast and manage it; split into sub-series.

---

## FM-15: WORLD EXPANSION WITHOUT NEED

**Definition:** Geography, factions, systems added without story function.

**Mechanism:** Each book adds worldbuilding to maintain interest. New regions never revisited; factions inert; bloat without payoff.

**Symptoms:** New location used in fewer than 2 scenes; faction named but no plot function; map grows but story stays in 3 locations; worldbuilding exceeds storytelling.

**At risk:** Saga; shared world; hybrid with world arc.

**Prevention:** World expansion must serve story. Every new region/faction must be used meaningfully. Consolidation passes.

**Recovery:** Stop expanding; consolidate; revisit existing territory with new depth; accept and prune.

---

## FM-16: FALSE RESET

**Definition:** Apparent change reversed to preserve status quo.

**Mechanism:** Major change introduced (death, rupture, transformation) then undone to preserve the series's existing configuration.

**Symptoms:** Death reversed within 2 books; relationship rupture healed with no consequence; trauma "cured" off-screen; reader feels previous book "didn't matter."

**At risk:** Recurring/procedural (status quo pressure); hybrid with weak arc commitment; commercial pressure for "same show."

**Prevention:** Engine-Permanence Compatibility Check: if the engine requires the protagonist to remain functionally unchanged, Character Permanence (Axis 4) must be Static or Accreting — don't fake change if the engine demands stasis. Consequence discipline: every major event leaves permanent residue. "No reset" rule for protected assets.

**Recovery:** Let the change stick and adapt the series; acknowledge the reset was a mistake and commit going forward.

## FAILURE MODE INTERACTIONS

Failure modes interact in chains:
- Premature climax → sequel inflation (trying to top the premature peak)
- Premature climax → endless middle (avoiding real climax because false one already happened)
- Canon overload → accidental dependency (more canon = more dependency)
- Protagonist exhaustion → repetitive sequels (without dynamic protagonist, books default to formula)
- Status-quo paralysis → premise exhaustion (nothing changes, premise explored and done)
- Mythology inflation → canon overload (more mythology = more canon)
- Reader-promise drift → premise exhaustion (new promise may not be as generative)
- Extension beyond natural ending → repetitive sequels + premise exhaustion (running on empty)

When diagnosing, look for chains, not just single modes.
