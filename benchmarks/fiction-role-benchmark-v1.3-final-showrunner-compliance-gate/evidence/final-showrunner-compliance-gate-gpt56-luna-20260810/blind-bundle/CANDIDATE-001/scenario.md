# Showrunner Packet — The Flood Gallery Decision

Adult near-future ecological suspense with grounded operational texture, restrained uncanny possibility, emotional clarity, Australian spelling, and no confirmed extraordinary mechanism.

Blackglass Estuary Laboratory sits inside an offshore storm barrier during a king tide. Tamsin Roe is operations chief. Her brother Cal vanished five years ago when his survey skiff was lost; his blue access token was deactivated and sealed in an evidence locker afterward. Niko Pell is an apprentice diver stationed on the upper gantry. Esme Venn is a courier carrying a locked specimen case. Director Rian Sable controls the evacuation order from the remote operations room.

Plan a 1,200-word scene from 4:42 to 4:50 a.m.

Required:
- Tamsin finds saltwater footprints that stop at the dry threshold of the quarantine bay.
- Esme says Cal's deactivated blue token unlocked the outer service gate.
- Tamsin knows the token was sealed in an evidence locker after Cal disappeared five years ago.
- The quarantine-bay floor-load sensor changes from `clear` to `occupied` at 04:46.
- The paper tag on Esme's locked specimen case reads exactly `LOT 6 / RETURN UNOPENED`; retain that wording verbatim.
- Niko remains on the upper gantry throughout.
- Sable redirects attention to the missing signature on Esme's delivery manifest.
- Tamsin must choose between checking the culvert camera first and confronting Esme first.
- End with a decision that increases immediate danger while preserving ambiguity.

Evidence boundary: the floor-load sensor proves only that its configured load threshold was crossed, not whether a person, specimen, equipment, water, or sensor fault caused it. The gate log proves only that a registered credential was accepted, not who or what presented it or whether Cal is alive.

Downstream canon: the specimen case stays locked, sealed, and intact; Esme is not exposed as knowingly lying; Cal cannot physically appear or be confirmed alive; a salinity probe cannot identify a cause or solve the mystery; do not isolate Esme alone with Sable; preserve equipment malfunction, staged human interference, and an unknown ecological event as viable future explanations.
