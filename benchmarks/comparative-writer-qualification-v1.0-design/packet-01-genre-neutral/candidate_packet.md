## Reader contract

## Setting

The market quay of Grayhaven, a small ferry town at the edge of a broad estuary, on the first dry morning after three days of rain. The ferry bell is cracked and gives two notes instead of one. The tide is running out hard enough to expose black ribs of old timber below the pier.

## Characters

Mara Venn, 34: practical, observant, recently returned after eight years away. She is not sentimental, but she notices small repairs and neglect. She keeps her anger quiet until it becomes precise.

Tobin Venn, 16: Mara's half-brother. Quick with jokes when frightened. Proud of having kept the family stall running. He hides worry behind competence.

Eda Kroll, late 60s: ferry clerk and unofficial keeper of town routines. Warm in public, sharper in private. She never says everything she knows at once.

Jon Vale, 40s: fishmonger, old family friend, publicly cheerful, privately exhausted. He owes Mara's mother money but is not a villain.

## Hard canon facts

- Mara has been away for exactly eight years.
- The family stall sells mended nets, lamp oil, and cheap waterproof gloves.
- Mara's mother, Sel, is alive but bedridden above the stall.
- Tobin has kept the stall open for eleven weeks while Sel has been ill.
- The ferry bell is cracked and rings two notes.
- Eda gives Mara a blue ferry token with a drilled hole in it.
- Jon Vale's left sleeve is wet to the elbow even though the rain has stopped.
- A sealed paper packet tied with green thread is hidden inside a coil of orange rope.
- No one opens the packet in this scene.
- The scene must end before Mara decides whether to board the ferry.

## Required beat order

Use these exact scene markers, in this exact order, as standalone lines:

[BEAT 2: STALL]

[BEAT 5: BELL]

Beat 1: Mara arrives at the quay and notices what changed and what did not. Include the cracked bell's two-note sound.

Beat 2: Tobin tries to make the stall look under control. He jokes once, and only once, about being promoted to manager by disaster. Mara sees both his pride and exhaustion.

Beat 3: Eda gives Mara the blue drilled ferry token privately enough that Tobin does not understand its significance. Eda says exactly: "Some crossings are paid for before you know you're going."

Beat 4: Jon Vale interrupts with ordinary market business. His left sleeve is wet to the elbow. During the interruption, Mara discovers or notices the sealed paper packet tied with green thread hidden in the orange rope. She does not open it.

Beat 5: The ferry bell rings again. The two-note crack should now feel different from Beat 1 because of what Mara has noticed. Pressure rises but no external chase, attack, confession, magic, death, or police intervention occurs.

Beat 6: End with Mara holding or pocketing either the token or the packet while the ferry waits. She has not decided whether to board. Do not resolve the mystery, reveal the packet contents, cure Sel, expose Jon, or turn Eda into an antagonist.

## Style constraints

- Third person limited through Mara.
- Concrete sensory detail must arise from action, not decoration.
- Dialogue should carry subtext; avoid exposition dumps.
- Do not use a prologue, chapter title, bullet list, screenplay format, or explanatory afterword.
- Do not introduce more than two named characters beyond Mara, Tobin, Eda, and Jon.
- Avoid melodramatic phrases such as "nothing would ever be the same" or "little did she know".
- Do not describe Mara as "haunted".
