# Night Shift Book 1 — Preservation List

**Purpose:** Record what the pipeline must protect before beginning any diagnosis or future revision.
**Date:** 2026-08-11
**Model:** GLM-5.2:cloud (ollama-cloud) — see Source Lock for runtime transition record

## Narrative Voice

- Third-person limited, closely following Mara Chen's perspective.
- The prose has a distinctive rhythmic, incantatory quality — short declarative sentences layered against longer observational passages. This rhythm mirrors the hospital's routine (handover, rounds, vitals) and the night shift's altered consciousness.
- The voice deliberately blurs interior and exterior — Mara's observations about the ward are inseparable from her emotional state. This is intentional, not a defect.
- Clinical/documentary precision in medical scenes (drug names, doses, vitals, procedures) creates a grounded realism that makes the supernatural elements more unsettling by contrast.
- The narrative repeatedly uses "She wrote what she saw. She did not write what she felt." as a structural refrain. This is load-bearing — it frames the tension between Mara's professional discipline and her growing awareness.

## POV Structure

- Single POV: Mara Chen throughout.
- No POV shifts to other characters. This is intentional — the reader only knows what Mara knows, and Mara deliberately refuses to know.
- Dreams and hallucinations are distinguished by context but not by formatting — the dream sequences in Ch 8 and Ch 11 flow naturally from waking scenes, mirroring Mara's blurring sense of reality.

## Core Ambiguity Architecture

- The novel is built on genuine ambiguity: every supernatural event has a plausible rational explanation (exhaustion, electrical faults, old pipes, hypoxia, post-anaesthetic confusion, dementia, microsleep).
- The existing Developmental Revision Plan (DEVELOPMENTAL_REVISION_PLAN.md) established "Option A — genuine ambiguity" with 60-70% supernatural emotional weight. This is the locked truth architecture per Andrew/Arden.
- The narration NEVER independently confirms the supernatural. Everything is filtered through Mara's perception, exhaustion, and psychological state.
- The novel must support at least three readings: (1) supernatural/ghost, (2) exhaustion/psychological, (3) ambiguous/unknowable.
- The final call light in Ch 16 must remain genuinely ambiguous — Hattie calling, phantom sound from exhaustion, or a nurse who can't stop hearing call lights.

## Protected Character Relationships

- Mara and Gwen: mentor/mentee evolving into peer/successor. Gwen's guilt and Mara's emergence must both be preserved. Neither character should be reduced to pure authority figure or pure vessel.
- Mara and her mother: the emotional core. The parallel between holding (nursing) and losing yourself. The fear of becoming her mother.
- Mara and Liam: teacher/student. Liam's growth from terrified student to someone who acts must be preserved. He is not just the scared character — he's the one who drew up lorazepam in the dark.
- Mara and Aunty Dot: the catalyst. Aunty Dot is not a exposition device — she's a person who is dying and knows it and chooses to share what she knows.
- Mara and the building/door: the central relationship. Not Mara and Hattie (they never interact directly in confirmed reality) — Mara and the question itself.

## Deliberate Unresolved Questions

These must remain unresolved:

1. Is Hattie Burns real (as a ghost/entity) or is she a pattern Mara's exhausted mind constructs from stories?
2. Is the door supernatural or an old foundation door with a draft?
3. Did Mara's pushing the door cause the power to come back, or did a breaker reset?
4. Did Aunty Dot's phone say "thank you" or was it static/air conditioning/the brain providing what the heart required?
5. Is the hum supernatural or the building's infrastructure?
6. Is Mara becoming her mother, or is she doing something different by choosing to hold AND having people to share the holding with?
7. Will Liam come back or move on?
8. Is Mr. Franklin's man in the corner the same entity as the thing behind the door, or something different?

## Tonal Characteristics

- Medical realism: precise, grounded, authentic nursing detail. This is the novel's anchor.
- Slow-burn dread: the horror builds through accumulation, not jump scares. Each chapter adds one piece.
- Australian specificity: Adelaide, Portrush Road, Prospect, the Hills, the heat, the light. Not generic hospital.
- Working-class texture: financial pressure, shift work, the body's response to sleep deprivation.
- Warmth within horror: Aunty Dot's humour, the lamingtons, the garden, Gwen's kindness to confused patients.
- The "watch" (3am-5am): the novel's signature atmospheric contribution. Must not be flattened.

## Structural Choices Known to Be Intentional

1. Chapters 1-6 are deliberately slow — the building and Mara's relationship with it develop through routine, not incident.
2. Chapter 7 (Aunty Dot's death) is the emotional turning point, not the plot turning point.
3. Chapter 12 is deliberately NOT a climax — Mara steps back. This is intentional per the revision plan: "discovery and refusal, not climax."
4. Chapter 13 is the true climax — the crisis forces the choice. The choice is made under pressure, not contemplation.
5. Chapters 14-16 are deliberately quiet — aftermath, not escalation. The emotional resolution is in the garden and the lamingtons, not the door.
6. The cross-chapter duplicated text between Ch 13 and Ch 16 (same drive-home sequences, same handover descriptions) may be intentional as structural rhyme — OR may be a GLM-5.2 generation artifact. This requires diagnosis, not assumption.
