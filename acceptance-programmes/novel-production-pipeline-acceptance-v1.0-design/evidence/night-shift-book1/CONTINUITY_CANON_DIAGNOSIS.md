## Findings

### CON-01: Room 14 death count inconsistency
- **Severity:** MEDIUM
- **Confidence:** HIGH
- **Location:** Ch 4 (lines 1012-1027) vs Ch 11 (line 2229) vs Ch 14 (line 2819)
- **Evidence:**
  - Ch 4: Mara searches the system and finds "four patients in the last six months" who died in Room 14. She then checks previous periods: "three" in the prior six months, "two" before that, "three" before that. Total: "Twelve deaths in two years."
  - Ch 11: Gwen says "eleven patients died in Room 14 between three and five" in 20 years.
  - Ch 14: Gwen says "eleven patients died in Room 14."
- **Contradiction:** Ch 4 establishes 12 deaths in 2 years. Ch 11/14 says 11 deaths in 20 years. These cannot both be true. If 12 died in 2 years, the 20-year total should be much higher. If only 11 died in 20 years, the 2-year rate of 12 is impossible.
- **Likely cause:** GLM-5.2 generated the Ch 4 death statistics independently from the Ch 11 Gwen confession. The numbers were not cross-checked during generation.
- **Canon impact:** This is an internal contradiction, not a series-canon conflict. The revision plan should reconcile: either the Ch 4 numbers are wrong (too high for a 20-year total of 11) or the Ch 11 number is wrong (too low for a 2-year rate of 12).
- **Proposed action:** Flag for revision. The revision plan does not explicitly address this. Recommend reconciling to a consistent number: likely 2-3 deaths per year (matching 12 in 2 years would give ~40 in 20 years, but Gwen says 11; the Ch 4 numbers seem too high for the 20-year total).

### CON-03: Aunty Dot's knowledge scope inconsistency
- **Severity:** MEDIUM
- **Confidence:** HIGH
- **Location:** Ch 3 (lines 658-668, 714-728) vs Ch 7 (lines 1493-1537)
- **Evidence:**
  - Ch 3: Aunty Dot says her mother "worked right here" as a cleaner, mentions the fire, says "a nurse died," says "the nurse is still here." She does NOT know the nurse's name or specific details.
  - Ch 7: Aunty Dot now knows the name "Hattie Burns," knows she was "a night nurse, like you," knows she "worked the three-to-five," knows she was "holding something shut when she died," and knows about "a door in the sub-basement."
- **Contradiction:** In Ch 3, Aunty Dot doesn't know the nurse's name. In Ch 7, she does. Where did she learn it? The novel doesn't explain this knowledge gain. She hasn't left the ward. She couldn't have read the archive (Mara did that). Possible explanation: she learned it from Mara's visits (Mara could have mentioned Hattie's name), but this isn't shown on-page.
- **Canon impact:** This is both a continuity issue and a developmental issue (the revision plan's Priority 2 calls for fragmenting Aunty Dot's knowledge so she knows LESS, not more). The current text has her gaining knowledge that isn't accounted for.
- **Proposed action:** Flag for revision. The revision plan's approach (Aunty Dot knows fragments, not the complete picture) would resolve this if applied consistently.

### CON-05: Liam's handprint injury — inconsistent description
- **Severity:** MEDIUM
- **Confidence:** MEDIUM
- **Location:** Ch 13 (line 2635) vs Ch 14 (line 2769)
- **Evidence:**
  - Ch 13: "The handprint on his forearm — the mark he had gotten in the dark corridor when something grabbed him or when he fell against a pipe or when he grabbed a cold metal rail in the dark"
  - Ch 14: "The mark on his forearm had faded to a faint red line, like a scratch, like a bruise, like the trace of something that had been there and was now gone."
  - Issue: The injury is described as a "handprint" in Ch 13 but the mechanism is uncertain (grabbed vs. fell against pipe vs. grabbed rail). The revision plan (Ch 9 notes) calls for adding a mundane alternative (cold-metal contact injury from basement pipes). But the injury event itself is never shown on-page — it's referenced only in retrospect. The reader doesn't see HOW Liam got the mark.
- **Canon impact:** The injury is mentioned but never dramatized. This is a gap — the reader is told about an event (Liam being grabbed/injured in the dark) that should have been shown.
- **Proposed action:** Flag for revision. Either dramatize the injury event or remove the handprint element. The revision plan's approach (add mundane alternative) is correct but the event needs to be shown, not just referenced.

### CON-06: The "missing time" motif introduced in Ch 1 but not developed
- **Severity:** LOW
- **Confidence:** MEDIUM
- **Location:** Ch 1 (line 608-612), Ch 2 (line 608)
- **Evidence:**
  - Ch 1: "She didn't lose time today. She remembered every minute of the drive"
  - Ch 2: Same line repeated: "She didn't lose time today. She remembered every minute of the drive"
  - The "missing time" concept is introduced as something Mara tracks (did she lose time on the drive home?) but it's only mentioned in Ch 1 and 2, then disappears. By Ch 13 and 16, the "remembered every minute" motif is used as a positive sign (she's present, not losing time).
  - Issue: The missing-time motif is introduced as potentially supernatural (losing time = something happened during the blank) but is never developed. It resolves implicitly (she stops losing time as the novel progresses) but this is never dramatized.
- **Canon impact:** Not a contradiction, but an underdeveloped thread. The revision plan does not address this.
- **Proposed action:** Observation — watch item for revision. May be intentional (the missing time resolves as Mara becomes more present/less exhausted).

- **Evidence:**

This is internally consistent — Gwen's "a month" in Ch 1 is about her total time on nights (which is a week at that point, but Gwen rounds up to establish authority). By Ch 13, she's been on nights for 4+ weeks. No contradiction.
